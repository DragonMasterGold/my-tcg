My TCG How To Play

Introduction:

Welcome to my currently unnamed Competitive TCG. A card game focused on preparation, planning, and decision-making. Favoring the player who masters these skills.


Core Principles:

If you’re already familiar with Trading Card Games, and especially Yu-Gi-Oh! then you can skip over this guide (for now) and read the rulebook to jump straight into playing faster.


Initial Overview:

There are three main Card Types.

Spirit Cards:

Spirits can be played onto the field during your turn, to activate their Ability. For example if you played “Cycle” which has the Ability “Draw 1.” You would play it, the Ability would Activate, then you would Draw 1 Card.

Spirit Abilities can only be activated on your turn.

Spirits are sent to the After Life after being used.

 

Counter Cards:

Counters are played face down onto the field, and cannot be activated the turn they are set. After that first turn they can be activated on either Players turn to use their Ability.

Counters are also sent to the AL (After Life) after being used.

 

Phantom Cards:

Phantoms are the Main Card type. These Cards have Attack and Health. When their Health is reduced to zero they Break and are sent to the After Life.

 

Phantoms have a Summoning Point (SP) cost to play them. Which is equal to their level.

Phantoms can be Unsummoned, on your Turn, to gain back their SP.

 

The default format has each player start with 4-SP each turn.


Duels:

The main win condition is to reduce the Opponent’s Life Points to 0.

Players start with 10,000 Life Points.

One Player goes 1st –– the other goes 2nd.

Both Players start with 5 Cards.

Both Players can Mulligan their Cards.

After the Mulligan Stage the Duel Starts.

 

Player 1 takes their Turn.

After Player 1’s Turn, is the Draw Phase. Both Players Draw 1 Card during the Draw Step.

Then Player 2 takes their turn.

 

Once Player 2’s Turn ends, the Round also Ends.

 

When a Player’s turn starts, their Summoning Points (SP) are set to 4. Regardless of if they had more-or-less prior.

There are no limits on Cards that can be played, besides your resources.

 

Phantoms can be played in Attack or Defense position.

Phantom Cards can be played Face Up or Face Down in either position.

While Face Down Cards cannot activate their Abilities.

 

Phantoms cannot the Attack the Opponent Directly the turn they are summoned.

They can Attack/Battle other Phantoms.

Phantoms cannot Attack Directly, if there is an Enemy Phantom on the Field.

 

Phantoms can only Attack while in Attack Position.

They can Target any Enemy Phantoms to Attack.

Successfully Attacking another Phantom has them enter into Battle.

In Battle Both Phantoms’ Attacks get deducted from the opposing Phantom’s Health.

 

When an Attack Position Phantom is Battled, any Damage dealt beyond their Health total, becomes Summoner Battle Damage, being dealt to the controlling Player.

You do not take Summoner Battle Damage if you initiate the Battle.

 

Defense Position Phantoms do not pass on Excess Battle Damage to their Player.

Defense Position Phantoms cannot initiate an Attack.

(Battle calculations work the same regardless of Attack or Defense Position).

 

You cannot Unsummon a Phantom the turn it has Battled.

But you can Unsummon a Phantom that only has Attacked or Direct Attacked.

Abilities can over write core game rules.

 

A Special Summon is a Summon that does not cost SP to perform.

You can Special Summon from your Hand or Extra Deck.

 

Phantom Abilities can be activated on either Player’s Turn.

Abilities by default can only be activated Once Per Turn, per Summon/Play of a Card.

 

Both Spirit and Counter Cards can be placed Face Down onto the Field and kept there.

 

Counters with a Condition can activated at any point, but only when their condition is met.

 

All Cards belong to Archetypes. Cards list what Archetypes they belong to. Which tells you what Abilities work with them.

Other Card Types:

Environment: Each player has an Environment Field Slot, which an Environment Card can be placed in. Environment Cards effect and can be used by both players when only one is on the field. When two are on the field, only the Environment card on your side of the field effects only you and your cards.

 


Field Slots:

The top 3 Card slots are for Phantoms.

The 3 Back Row slots are for Support Cards (Spirits/Counters).

With 2 Balance Slots on either side, where Phantoms, Spirits, and Counters can be played into.

1 Environment Card Slot.

 

1 Deck Slot.

1 Side Deck Slot.

1 Extra Deck Slot.

 

1 AL (After Life) Slot.

1 Shadow Realm Slot.

1 Oblivion Slot.

 

1 Environment Slot.


 

 

Deck Building:

Deck building vital to determining who will win or lose.

However, Deck isn’t quite right, because you have multiple Decks.

The Main Deck, which you will draw from.

The Side Deck, which you can access between Duels and with specific Abilities.

And the Extra Deck; which consists of only Phantoms.

 

You can add up to 3-copies per card to any of these Decks.

The Main Deck is minimally 40 Cards –– and a Maximum of 60 Cards.

The Side Deck can be 0-20 Cards.

The Extra Deck can be 0-10 Phantom Cards.

 

When Playing a Match with multiple duels, such as best of 3, you can freely move cards between your three decks between Duels.

But you cannot add in new Cards or entirely remove Cards from your Decks.


 

 

Extra Deck Mechanics:

On your turn, you can spend 4-SP to Extract a Card from your Extra Deck (adding it to your hand).

You can also spend 2-SP to Exchange a Phantom from your hand with one from your Extra Deck.

 

Draw Phase – Reserve Draw:

After the Draw Step, before the Draw Phase Ends is the Reserve Draw Step.

Both Players can spend SP to Draw additional Cards.

 

If you have less than 4 Cards in hand, you can Spend 2-SP to Draw a card.

If you have 4 or more Cards in hand, you can Spend 4-SP to Draw a card.

 

You can draw until you run out of SP.

 

After both players have had a chance to Reserve Draw, the Draw Phase Ends.

 

 


 

Mulligan System:

At the start of the Duel, both Players Draw 5 cards from their Main Deck.

Then both Players Mulligan.

You can any number of Cards to Mulligan, by placing them to the side.

Then Draw that same number of cards.

You then choose to keep either the first set of set aside Mulligan Cards, or the newly drawn ones.

Add the selected group to your hand. Shuffle back the unselected into the Deck.

 

Full Mulligan Mechanics:

If you Shuffle back all 5 of your set aside starting hand Cards during the Mulligan, you can decide to Full Mulligan.

Doing so lets you Mulligan your newly Drawn Cards as normal.

After doing so, you then must pick one of the 5 Cards from your hand to Shuffle back into the Deck after Mulligining.

 

 

Full Mulligan Going 2nd:

The going 2nd Player can do a Full Mulligan without Shuffling back a Card after.

They can also Full Mulligan a second time, but must Shuffle back 1 Card after Mulliganing.


 

 

Going 2nd Equalizers:

Going 2nd Extract/Exchange Equalizer:

The very first Extract or Exchange the 2nd Player uses –– costs half the SP.

 

Extract Costs 2-SP.

Exchange Costs 1-SP.

 

They only get the discount for one or the other. And only for the first Extract/Exchange.

 

 

Going 2nd Reserve Draw Equalizer:

The very first Reserve Draw the Going 2nd Player uses –– costs half the SP.

4-SP becomes 2.

2-SP becomes 1.

 


 

 

Turn Structure:

Each Turn has 3 Stages: Turn Start, the Turn itself, and Turn End.

At the start and at the end of each of these stages the Turn Player has Priority to act first. Such as by activating an Ability.

Once the Turn player has passed Priority, the going 2nd Player gets Priority to act.

 

The Draw Phase also has 4 Stages: Draw Phase Start, Draw Step, Reserved Draw Step, Draw Phase End.

During the Draw Phase the going 2nd Player has Priority.

And once passed Priority goes to the going 1st Player to act.

 

Whenever a Player takes a Game Action the Opponent is given Priority to act / respond.

If they then take a Game Action the Priority passes back.

 


 

 

Lines:

You can react to your own cards or your opponent’s cards with your Abilities.

And those Abilities can be reacted to as well.

When this happens it forms what is called a Line.

Each new Ability activated becomes a part of the Line.

Each new Ability added to the line becomes the next Line Step.

The first Ability is Step 1, then the next is Step 2, then Step 3, Step 4, and so on.

Each activated Ability extends the Line. Once Abilities have stopped being added to the Line the already added Abilities will activate in order from newest added to oldest added.

Once a Line starts resolving, neither player can activate their cards in response to it.

Mandatory card Abilities are added to another Line after the current Line Resolves.

  

Ability Speeds:

Different actions, Cards, and Abilities have different Speeds.

 

A regular Game Action the player takes, such as playing a card, or declaring an attack, is Speed 1.

Phantom Card and Spirit Card Abilities are default Speed 2.

Counter Card Abilities are by default Speed 3.

 

Certain Universal Game Mechanics, such as Unsummoning as a Game Action, are taken at Speed 0, which means they cannot be directly responded to. (But priority passes to the Opponent after the action resolves).

 

Some Abilities allow Cards to be played at higher-or-lower speeds.

 

A card/Ability must be of equal or higher Speed to be activated in response to another card.

You can only react to the last Ability activated.

You cannot react to Abilities that came earlier in the Line.

Speed 0 cannot react to anything including other Speed 0 actions.


Line Break:

When an Ability of a higher speed is activated in response to a lower Speed Ability, what is called a Line Break occurs.

After a Line Break, a lower Speed Ability than the current new line cannot be activated.

Only Abilities of the current Line Speed or higher can be Activated.

 

For example:

Player 1 has a Phantom declares an Attack.

Player 2 Activates a Phantom Ability. This action makes a Line Break and starts a new Line and becomes the second Step in the line.

Player 1 Activates a Spirit Ability, becoming Step 3.

Player 2 Activates a Counter. This counter causes a Line Break.

Now only Speed 2 or better Abilities can be Activated in response.

 

A Line Break has the card that caused the Line Break become Step 1 of the new Line.

Each Line is resolved in order of Speed, following the Steps from highest to lowest.

 

When a Line is fully resolved up to where the Line Break occurred, the highest numbered step can be reacted to again.

Any new Ability activations are added onto that Line. However, just as normal, once a Line starts resolving you can no longer activate any card in response.

Not until another Line Break, where you can only activate a card in response to the highest step in that current Line.
